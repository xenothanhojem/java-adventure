8.1 Economic Reasons for Using Computers 
The computer does things quickly and more accurately than the alternate option of doing the task 
manually. The computer is able to carry out tasks accurately and is more reliable than the manual 
alternative, provided, of course, that the user feeds the computer accurate data. 
Computers streamline operations in businesses and make globalised businesses possible through 
providing effortless communication using the Internet. Business communication has become almost 
completely paperless. This is good for our environment. 
Computing has not only allowed the efficient, accurate and reliable computation of complex 
calculations, it also allows us to store extremely large amounts of data with effortless retrieval. This in 
turn saves paper and makes the processing of these large amounts of data accurate and reliable. 
Productivity in industry is much greater, more cost effective, more efficient and more reliable when 
computers are used in manufacturing; replacing manual labour.  
8.2 Digital Divide 
The term digital divide refers to the gap between the: haves” 
and the “have nots”. The “haves” are classified as those who 
have regular, effective access to digital and information 
technology, and the “have nots” obviously do not. While it is 
clearly an issue about access to computers and the Internet, 
it is also the accompanying skills and resources which enable 
the technology to be used. Groups often discussed in the 
context of a digital divide include socioeconomic (rich/poor), 
racial (white/minority), or geographical (urban/rural).

Digitally enabled citizens are those that have access to technology and can communicate globally 
using e-Communications. They have a set of skills which they can use to become productive 
members of society.

8.2.1.2 Poverty 
Along with the lack of infrastructure is the fact that most countries are extremely poor. The 
governments do not have the finances to provide the infrastructure, schools and basic necessities.  
8.2.1.3 Education 
A lack of education, basic literacy and schooling facilities is another contributing factor. It is probably 
one of the easier to overcome provided that the technology exists. Dr Sugata Mitre has proved that 
children can learn by themselves given access to computers and fast Internet access. Without 
technology, the education system is reliant on teachers, textbooks and classrooms.

8.2.2 Bridging the Divide 
Closing the gap between those who have access to technology and those who do not, is not a simple 
solution. The answer lies in a combination of political stability, improved infrastructure, reduced 
poverty and improved education. Cell phones have helped to reduce the gap since their use has 
increased in Africa faster than the use of computers. Most phones provide Internet connectivity and 
are used in social networking. These features enable users to be connected and have access to 
information and other services. Through mobile service cell phones can be used to produce more 
digitally enabled citizens in Africa and other deprived areas.

8.3.2 Repetitive Strain Injuries (RSI)  
Repetitive Strain Injuries occur from repeated physical 
movements, like using the keyboard or the mouse, doing 
damage to tendons, nerves, muscles, and other soft body 
tissues. Common symptoms are tightness, discomfort, tingling, 
coldness, numbness, stiffness, soreness, burning or pain in the 
hands, wrists, fingers, forearms, elbows, upper back, shoulders, 
or neck. You are more likely to develop problems if you use 
incorrect typing techniques or body positions that place 
unnecessary stress on muscles. While you are typing your 
wrists should not rest on anything and should not be bent to the 
side.

8.3.3 Carpal Tunnel Syndrome (CTS)  
Carpal Tunnel Syndrome is a painful condition involving the 
median nerve and the flexor tendons that extend from the 
forearm into the hand through a “tunnel” made up of the wrist 
bones, or carpals, and the transverse carpal ligament. As you 
move your hand and fingers, the flexor tendons rub against the 
sides of the tunnel. This rubbing can cause irritation of the 
tendons, causing them to swell. When the tendons swell, they 
apply pressure to the median nerve. The result can be tingling, 
numbness, and eventually debilitating pain. It can be treated 
with splints or braces, anti-inflammatory medications, cortisone 
injections or surgery to reduce the swelling and pressure on 
the median nerve.

8.4 Green Computing 
8.4.1 Electrical Consumption 
Computer systems account for between 5 and 10 percent of the world's electricity usage. Much of this 
energy is wasted since many people leave their computers on even when not in use. If one computer 
is left on in sleep mode a very small amount of power is being used. However, collectively a large 
number of computers left in sleep mode are using a great deal of power collectively.

8.4.2 Green Disposal 
The second major area of green computing is recycling and reusing. Before buying a new computer 
or device consider upgrading some of the components to extend the life of device.  
Computer components must be recycled. Locate a reliable IT waste disposal centre in your area and 
make use of it for the recycling of any electrical, computer and electronic components. Printer 
cartridges which cannot be refilled should also be disposed of at a print cartridge store who take in old 
cartridges or at an IT waste disposal centre. Batteries should also be disposed of responsibly at an 
appropriate disposal centre. Try to reduce the amount you print and recycle the unwanted paper that 
you use for printing.  
When purchasing computer equipment check for design energy efficient computers, servers, printers 
and electronic devices. 
Also check the manufacturing company's green manufacturing policy and their commitment to 
minimise waste during manufacturing of computers and devices.
